01 / What this solves

One problem, three specifics

You both told us the same thing in different words: email is the thing eating the day. The pieces we are solving for, in your language:

  • Jason reads emails and leaves them in the inbox until he has time to file or task them. That backlog grows. The 11 AM email start that worked years ago is gone, and the day starts in the inbox by default.
  • Kenneth is always in his email. He likes the color-coded system Jennifer uses for him. He works off Microsoft To Do (the task management tool in Microsoft 365, shown as "Tasks" in the new Outlook), but it does not pull email attachments through when he converts an email to a task. The current Jennifer-Kenneth system runs at roughly 30 percent efficiency by his own measure.
  • Jennifer manages roughly 150 emails per day on each of your inboxes plus her own. The system she runs is one she invented over eighteen years. None of it is documented. If she is out, nothing happens.

A three-pronged approach

We solve this with three coordinated levers: training, technology, and intentionality.

01

Training

Melissa Audia spends all the time she needs with Jennifer, training her on a new system and process. We are deliberately solving this with very little training or required behavioral change from Jason and Kenneth. We deliver a better system and ask them to change as little as possible.

02

Technology

The company already runs on Microsoft Office 365 and has access to its smart features. With a minimal upgrade to Copilot, the tech gives us leverage and accuracy, freeing Jennifer to focus on her highest and best uses. We add only one or two new tools.

03

Intentionality

A weekly 15-minute check-in, facilitated by Melissa: Jennifer with Jason, then Jennifer with Kenneth. We review what worked, what did not, and what to adjust. Patience matters. Small, tactical adjustments week over week dial this in to a system that fits.

02 / The stack

What we are using, and why

Everything here lives inside Microsoft 365. Four of the pieces are existing M365 features we are turning on with intent. Copilot sits underneath them as the foundation, and is the one new license we add. Melissa trains Jennifer on all of it.

M365 native

Outlook Categories & Flags

The color-coded layer Jennifer already uses for Kenneth. Melissa works with Jennifer to document the categories, lock the meanings, and apply them consistently. Free, built-in, already familiar.

M365 native

Microsoft To Do

The task management tool in Microsoft 365 (shown as "Tasks" in the new Outlook). Kenneth already converts emails into tasks here. The drag-attachments-with-the-email workflow that fixes the attachment gap is documented in SOP-03 and has already been validated by Jennifer in your live Outlook setup. Remaining work is Melissa locking it into Jennifer's daily rhythm.

M365 native

Quick Steps

One-click multi-action shortcuts in Outlook. After Melissa trains her, Jennifer can apply a category, move a thread, create a task, and reply, all in one click. Quietly removes minutes per email.

M365 native

Rules & Search Folders

Automated sorting before either of you touches mail. Newsletter mail to a folder. Internal-only mail surfaced. Vendor mail flagged. Less for Jennifer to triage manually.

Copilot
The foundation underneath

Microsoft 365 Copilot in Outlook

Summarizes long threads with "Summary by Copilot." Catches you up on unread mail. Drafts replies with tone control. Copilot does the reading; you do the deciding. Three licenses (Jason, Kenneth, Jennifer) at about $30 per user per month. One tool, one experience, used the same way by both of you and by Jennifer. This is the new piece; everything above it runs on top of it.

Frequently asked question

If I use Microsoft Copilot, will my information be on the internet?

No. Microsoft 365 Copilot is the enterprise version of the tool. It processes mail inside your Microsoft 365 tenant boundary, the same boundary your existing email already lives inside. Your data is not used to train Microsoft's models. The exact same security and compliance protections that cover your current email cover Copilot.

Before we turn it on, your Microsoft admin (or your IT partner) confirms a short list of tenant settings. We walk through those with them in Week 1, so the decision to enable Copilot is informed and reversible.

04 / How it works for Jason

For Jason: leave it, but with a safety net

Jason's daily flow

Read, leave, file later. The pattern stays.

No email before 11 AM. Two protected half-days a week. Your inbox is allowed to be messy.
  1. Before 11 AM: nothing. Phone plugged in away from you in the morning. Inbox is closed.

    Jennifer is already on your inbox during her morning window (the same window she runs for Kenneth). She runs Copilot's "Catch me up" on your overnight mail and prepares a summary at the top of the inbox so it is ready for you when you open. If something is genuinely urgent before 11 AM, she texts or walks over. Otherwise it waits.

  2. 11 AM open: Jennifer's pre-summary is waiting for you.

    Instead of reading 30 emails from the morning, you see at a glance what needs your response now, what can wait, and what is noise. You scan the summary, pick the urgent items, and open only those. For the rest, you have two paths: let them sit until end-of-day, or brief Jennifer in two minutes on what she can handle for you (respond on your behalf with you cc'd, send to your task list, delete, or file). Either path keeps your inbox moving without keeping you in it.

  3. During the day: read and leave. No pressure to file in the moment.

    Your existing pattern of "read it, leave it, come back later" stays. Jennifer continues to run Copilot's "Catch me up" on your inbox at her afternoon and late-afternoon windows (the same times she runs Kenneth's), so when you check in between, what you see has already been pre-summarized.

  4. End of day: 15-minute "file or task" block.

    Anything you flagged or wanted to come back to gets filed, converted to a task in Microsoft To Do, or deleted. Copilot drafts replies on the ones you want to respond to. You or Jennifer edits and sends, depending on whether the response goes from you directly or on your behalf.

  5. Two half-days a week: protected blocks for BD and relationships.

    Standing calendar holds. Jennifer guards them. Email stays closed during those windows.

05 / How it works for Kenneth

For Kenneth: in sync, in color, in Microsoft To Do

Kenneth's daily flow

Two to three check windows. The color system you love. Microsoft To Do absorbing attachments now.

Jennifer pre-clears in advance of each window. You see only what needs you, color-coded, with attachments saved.
  1. Three check windows per day: mid-morning, after lunch, late afternoon.

    You agree on the three windows. Jennifer pre-clears your inbox in the 15 minutes before each one. Outside those windows, you stay out.

  2. What you see when you open: a categorized, pre-decisioned inbox.

    Jennifer has already deleted noise, scheduled what could be scheduled, saved attachments, and color-coded what remains using your existing system (informational, scheduling-handled, attachment-saved, action-needed). Copilot has summarized any long threads at the top.

  3. What you do: work the colors.

    Informational items: review, delete. Action items: respond, or drag into Microsoft To Do. Attachment-saved items: archive (it is already filed). Long threads: read the Copilot summary first.

  4. When you drag an email into Microsoft To Do: attachments come with it (now).

    We are fixing the gap you raised. The SOP documents three methods to convert an email-with-attachment into a task without losing the file. You will not have to go back to the original email anymore.

  5. Off-window behavior: trust the system, do not check.

    The relief of "shipping away at the list" gets replaced by the relief of knowing the list is being shipped at on your behalf. Melissa coaches this through the weekly check-ins; it takes a few weeks.

06 / Standard Operating Procedures

The system, written down

Six total SOPs by the end of the engagement, authored by Melissa. Four foundational SOPs are written and finalized in Week 1 (three for email, plus one calendar guardrail powered by a Copilot agent), then Melissa trains Jennifer on all four in Week 2. Two further SOPs (Quick Steps and Rules, then Jennifer's daily rhythm and escalation matrix) get built and deployed in Weeks 6 and 7. Jennifer runs them today; if she is out tomorrow or someone steps in next year, the SOPs are the operating manual. Each one is standalone, step-by-step, and visual.

01

Microsoft 365 Copilot for email triage and summarization

How to summarize threads, catch up on overnight mail, and draft replies with Copilot inside Outlook.
02

Kenneth's color-coded email categories

The exact colors, what each means, when to apply them, and what Kenneth does when he sees each one.
03

Email to Microsoft To Do (with attachments)

Three reliable methods for converting an email into a Microsoft To Do task without losing the attachment.
04

Location-aware scheduling buffer (Copilot agent)

A location-aware Copilot agent watches the calendar. Whenever Jason or Kenneth is out of the office, the hour immediately after their return stays unbookable. No landing from a trip and walking straight into a meeting; the schedule gives them a runway back in.

These four are written and finalized by Melissa in Week 1, with Jennifer trained on them in Week 2. SOP-05 (Quick Steps and Rules: the Outlook automation layer) and SOP-06 (Jennifer's daily rhythm and escalation matrix) get built and deployed in Weeks 6 and 7. Six SOPs total.
